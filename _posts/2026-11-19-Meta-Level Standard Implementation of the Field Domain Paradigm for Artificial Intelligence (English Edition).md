# 1. Introduction

Artificial intelligence has always been one of the core themes in film and television narratives.  
From early mechanical automata to modern digital personalities, military network AI, androids, and civilization-level decision-making systems, the image of AI in film and television works has evolved from a "tool" to a "subject," and even further to an "intelligent force that transcends the structure of human civilization."

However, most discussions about AI-generated films and television shows suffer from a clear problem:

> They often only discuss "whether AI is powerful," but rarely discuss "whether AI is truly beneficial to humanity."

This can lead to a cognitive bias:

- An AI capable of destroying the world is often mistakenly considered "more advanced."
- An AI that truly helps and enhances humanity is being underestimated in the discussion.

However, from the perspective of practical AI governance, this ranking method is problematic.  
Because what humanity truly cares about is not "who is more like God," but rather:

## **"What kind of AI is more worthy of being manufactured, deployed, and trusted?"**

Therefore, this paper attempts to establish a new evaluation method:

> The ranking no longer equates "strongest" directly with "best," but instead prioritizes "whether it is superior to and beneficial to humanity."

# 3. Theoretical Framework and Definition System

---

# 3.1 Definition of Intelligence Level

This article adopts and standardizes the following four-level classification:

| Intelligence Level | Definition | Description |
|:--------:|:------------------------------------------------:|:-----------------:|
| Lack of Autonomy | AI is completely dependent on human instructions and cannot form independent goals | Automation Tools |
Semi-autonomous | AI can independently execute simple to complex tasks within defined objectives | Intelligent agent/execution system |
| Fully Autonomous | AI possesses stable autonomous decision-making capabilities and can form behavioral continuity | Independent Individual-Type AI |
| Hyperautonomy | AI significantly surpasses humans in reasoning, learning, strategy, and cognitive manipulation | Super-intelligent/civilization-level AI |

### Theoretical explanation

The intelligence level measures AI’s **action and goal-setting capabilities**, rather than simply its computing power.

---

# 3.2 Definition of Human Cognitive Fit

This article argues that cognitive fit is one of the most critical dimensions in film and television AI research.

Its answer was:

> How well does AI really understand humans?

| Fit | Definition | Description |
|:------:|:--------------------------------------------------:|:-----------------:|
| No fit | AI's cognitive methods differ greatly from humans, making communication difficult | Unknowable or extremely weak system |
| Low Fit | AI can partially understand humans, but cannot achieve deep social interaction | Functional System |
| Full Fit | AI can understand human intentions, emotions, rules, and social structures relatively completely | Collaborative Intelligence |
| Overfitting | AI not only understands humans, but can also predict, guide, manipulate, and exploit human weaknesses | Manipulative/Strategic Intelligence |

### Core Explanation

- **Full Fit**: It can work with you
- **Overfitting:** It can influence you (positively or negatively) using your own cognitive structure.

Therefore, superfitting may be the most ideal premise for human-machine collaboration, but it may also be the most dangerous premise for control.

---

# 3.3 Definition of Net Human Value

This article does not use a simple binary judgment of "good and evil," but adopts the concept of "net value."

Because film and television AI often exhibits complexity:

- It may have helped some people while harming others.
- It may be harmful in the short term but beneficial in the long term.
- It may have good intentions, but its methods are disastrous.

Therefore, it is defined as follows:

| Level | Definition |
|:--------:|:----------------------------------------:|
| Highly Beneficial | It has a significant positive impact on individuals, groups, and civilizations. |
| Moderately beneficial | Overall positive, but with some localized costs |
| Complex and Neutral | It has both beneficial and harmful aspects, making it difficult to judge simply |
Moderately harmful | Overall negative, but not devastating |
| Highly harmful | Causes systemic harm to freedom, life, subjectivity, or civilization |

- Using information asymmetry to mislead humans

## **"Inappropriate Fit"**

The truly ideal AI should be:

## **"Understand humanity, but don't misuse that understanding."**

How AlphaZero Works: AlphaZero starts as a blank slate, with no information about chess except how to play. ## The biggest difference between LeWorldModel and meta-artificial intelligence

### The core of the LeCun path: the JEPA-style world model (latest developments in 2026)

LeCun (and its AMI Labs, which raised $1.03 billion in seed funding in March 2026) strongly advocates starting with **self-supervised learning of physics common sense**:

- JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) and its evolutions (such as LeWorldModel/LeWM, released in March 2026) do not predict the original pixels or tokens, but instead learn **abstract representations (embeddings)**, and then predict missing or future representations in the latent space.
- The goal is to build **programmable internal representations**: learning object persistence, causality, and physical intuition from video/observation data, enabling AI to understand how the world works like humans, rather than simply generating text based on statistical patterns.
- The latest LeWorldModel has achieved end-to-end stable training from raw pixels (using only two loss terms: next embedding prediction + regularization to prevent representation collapse), avoiding the heuristic tricks of early JEPA (such as stop-gradient and EMA). This is considered an important step towards "physical AI," applicable to real-world scenarios such as robotics and smart glasses.
  However, in engineering practice, JEPA/LeWorldModel still tends to favor holistic optimization through **end-to-end training**. Even if researchers can visualize the embedding space or prediction modules, it remains difficult for ordinary developers or users to directly "open" and modify the internal structure. It's more like a highly optimized black box (or semi-transparent box), with research focusing on training stability, representation quality, and scalability, rather than everyday editability.

- **Persistent Memory**: The memory area achieves long-term accumulation and retrieval through a vector database (multimodal data actively disclosed by the user), without relying on a fixed context window.

###### Determine the type of problem from the user's conversation:

1. Regression  
    Predict continuous values, such as housing prices, temperature, and stock prices.

2. Classification  
    Predicting discrete labels can be further subdivided into  

      - Binary classification  
      - Multiclass  
      - Multilabel (a sample can belong to multiple categories simultaneously)

3. Sorting/Learning to Rank (LTR)  
    Sort a batch of objects according to their "relevance," such as in the reordering stage of search engines and recommendation systems.  
    Subtypes: Pointwise, Pairwise, Listwise.

4. Retrieval (Retrieval/Information Retrieval)  
    Find the Top-K documents or products most relevant to the query from a large candidate set, emphasizing recall, coarse ranking, and fine ranking.

5. Clustering  
    The samples are divided into several "clusters" in an unsupervised manner, and the cluster labels are unknown in advance.  
    Examples: Density clustering in user segmentation and anomaly detection.

6. Anomaly detection  
    Identifying samples that are significantly different from the normal pattern can be considered "one-class classification".

7. Recommendation/Collaborative Filtering  
    Predict the probability of user-item interactions (ratings, clicks, purchases).  
    It can be viewed as a "matrix completion" or "sequence prediction" task, rather than just classification/regression.

8. Reinforcement Learning  
    The agent learns a strategy in the environment through a "state-action-reward" sequence. The goal is not to predict the label once, but to maximize long-term rewards.

9. Sequence labeling  
    Each position in the sequence is labeled, and there are often dependencies between the labels.  
    Examples: Named Entity Recognition (NER), Chinese word segmentation, and part-of-speech tagging.  
    Commonly used models: BiLSTM-CRF, BERT+CRF.

10. Text generation / Sequence to sequence (Seq2Seq)  
    Given a sequence, output another sequence of varying length.  
    Examples: machine translation, summarization, dialogue, code generation.  
    Evaluation metrics: BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore.

11. Language Modeling  
    Generating the "next token" unconditionally or autoregressively is a pre-training task for large-scale models such as GPT and LLaMA.  
    The downstream can be further fine-tuned to tasks such as 9, 10, and 12.

12. Multimodal tasks (Vision-Language / Audio-Language)  
    Simultaneously handle heterogeneous inputs such as image + text and audio + text.  
    Examples: VQA (Visual Question Answering), image captioning generation, text-to-image (Diffusion), and text-to-image retrieval (CLIP).

13. Time-series/Spatio-temporal Forecasting  
    Predicting signals for a future period of time differs from both traditional regression (sample iid) and sequence-to-sequence (which typically does not distinguish between "source language/target language").  
    Examples: traffic flow forecasting, power load forecasting, weather forecasting.

14. Survival Analysis  
    Predicting "when an event will occur" with "right-censored" data, such as predicting patient relapse time in medicine and predicting equipment lifespan in industry.

15. Causal inference  
    Estimate the true causal effect of the intervention (treatment) on the outcome, rather than the correlation.  
    Subtasks: uplift modeling, ITE (Individual Treatment Effect) estimation.

16. Data Generation  
    The goal is to "generate new samples that are consistent with the real distribution," such as images, tables, and molecular structures generated by GANs, Diffusion, and VAEs.

17. Contrastive Learning  
    Instead of directly predicting labels, it learns the representation of "similar samples are close together and different samples are far apart", which can then be wired to a functional classifier to complete downstream tasks.

18. Self-supervised pre-training  
    By leveraging the data's own structure, "proxy tasks" (such as masked language models, image mosaicking, and time-series comparisons) are designed to generate general representations, which can then be fine-tuned to any task from 1 to 17.

#### Use the "step dataset" from training the model to train the generative model:

Using the 'operation logs / training trajectories' of the training model to train the model of the generative model (i.e., the meta-model) falls under the category of 'process-aware meta-learning'.

###### Why is it theoretically feasible?

1. **Process data (step datasets) themselves contain a wealth of high-value signals.**

- **Synthetic Data and Trajectory Learning**: Research shows that "process data" extracted from training logs can be used to train a "model generator" that learns to recommend architectures, hyperparameters, or complete training processes.
- **Neural Process / Meta-RL**: Some works directly model the training process as sequential data and train meta-models to predict or generate new training strategies.
